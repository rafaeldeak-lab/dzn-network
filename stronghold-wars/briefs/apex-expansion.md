# DZN NETWORK — APEX LEGENDS EXPANSION BRIEF

## Addendum to the Clash of Clans inspired DZN plan

**Instruction for the work chat:** Continue the DZN project from the Clash-inspired brief already supplied. Add the systems below to that existing plan and implementation. Preserve the shared community base, construction economy, ordinary troops, sector warfare and calling-card collection. All DZN features below are design proposals; the Apex sections explain the reference game.

**The combined direction:** The community builds and develops its headquarters. Individual members choose distinctive operatives, lead tactical missions, contribute to shared wars and earn recognisable personal achievements. The community’s base gives everyone a shared purpose; operatives and cards give each member an identity within it.

Apex information was checked against official EA/Respawn material on **23 October 2026**. Exact damage values, cooldowns, loot tables and playlists remain update-dependent.

# PART 1 — APEX LEGENDS MECHANICS AND FEATURES

## 1. The main gameplay loop

Apex combines a squad shooter with battle royale survival:

1. Choose a character and form a squad.
2. Deploy into a map.
3. Acquire weapons, equipment and supplies.
4. Gather information and decide which fights to take.
5. Move as the safe area contracts.
6. Survive successive engagements and become the last surviving squad.

Players balance fighting, looting, healing and repositioning. A squad can lose a favourable fight by staying too long, running low on supplies or attracting another team.

Movement includes sprinting, sliding, climbing and using traversal tools such as ziplines. Cover, height, approach routes and escape routes affect whether a position is useful. Contextual pings help teammates communicate enemies, equipment and intended movement.

The shrinking ring creates spatial pressure and damages players outside it. A **rotation** is a move towards a better or safer position; a **third party** is another squad joining an existing fight.

Deployment rules vary: Jumpmaster deployment exists, while sufficiently high-ranked lobbies can use assigned Drop Zones.

**Design lesson:** Make preparation, timing, information and positioning matter alongside combat strength.

## 2. Legends, classes and abilities

Legends are distinct playable characters. Their identities combine a visual design, personality, combat role and ability kit.

Each has:

- **Passive:** an ongoing or condition-triggered trait.
- **Tactical:** a more frequently available active ability.
- **Ultimate:** a major ability whose timing can change an engagement.

The five classes are:

| Class | Main purpose |
|---|---|
| Assault | Apply combat pressure. |
| Recon | Find enemies and gather information. |
| Skirmisher | Engage, reposition and disengage. |
| Controller | Hold space and shape enemy movement. |
| Support | Sustain, assist and recover the squad. |

Core modes add class perks. Different kits support different team compositions, and characters can contribute through information, protection or recovery as well as damage. Freestyle modes can change these rules. Character kits are also reworked over time, so historical ability descriptions should not be treated as permanent.

**Design lesson:** Give players different useful jobs. Each powerful action should have understandable conditions, a visible effect and an opportunity for opponents to respond.

## 3. Weapons and equipment choices

The weapon families include assault rifles, submachine guns, light machine guns, marksman weapons, sniper rifles, shotguns and pistols.

Their usefulness varies with range, accuracy, handling, recoil, reload time and ammunition constraints. A weapon suitable for an open sightline may be awkward in a close interior. Attachments modify performance.

Current Core hop-ups begin attached but locked. Players earn points to activate them; the activated gun retains the benefit when someone else picks it up. Care Package weapons use their own equipment and ammunition rules. Owning a cosmetic weapon skin does not give the player that weapon at deployment.

Two current examples of equipment design:

- **Corrupted Attachments** pair a benefit with a drawback and are limited to one per weapon.
- **Energy weapons** now use a regenerating ammunition stockpile following the August 2026 changes; regeneration pauses while firing or reloading.

**Design lesson:** A useful equipment system offers tradeoffs. “More magazine capacity, slower reload” produces a decision; an item that improves everything usually becomes the obvious choice.

## 4. Health, shields and temporary combat progression

Health and shields are separate recovery concerns. Syringes and Med Kits restore health; Shield Cells and Batteries restore shields; a Phoenix Kit restores both. Healing costs time and creates an opportunity for opponents to push. Lootable Legend Amps add passive benefits.

Core play includes EVO-driven progression and Legend upgrade choices during a match. These upgrades reset between matches, separating the current run’s development from permanent account ownership.

Shield Cores hold shield charge. Swapping a core can replenish charge, and a higher-capacity core can grant temporary overcharge.

**Design lesson:** Players can grow stronger during an encounter without permanently stacking every advantage they have ever earned. Keep permanent unlocks, current equipment and temporary encounter upgrades understandable as separate systems.

## 5. Being downed, revived and respawned

A knocked teammate can be revived before full elimination. Eliminated teammates can return through recovery systems involving banners and Respawn Beacons. These create a decision: continue fighting, retreat, or risk recovering someone.

May 2026 added **Deathbox Respawns**, allowing a teammate to return at their deathbox. The interaction is conspicuous, restores gear that remains unlooted, and has restrictions after repeated deaths. **Chain Healing** also lets players queue subsequent healing items.

Recovery rules depend on the mode. Wildcard’s easier returns and healing should not be assumed to apply to ordinary Core play.

**Design lesson:** Defeat can create a rescue objective. Limited recovery gives support roles a meaningful job and lets a setback produce another decision.

## 6. Crafting and battlefield interactables

Replicators provide in-match crafting and recovery opportunities. The 2024 redesign removed the old crafting-material collection economy and made individual machines a limited personal opportunity. This is separate from Crafting Metals used for permanent cosmetics.

Supply opportunities such as Care Packages and Arsenals create reasons to visit or contest particular locations. The August 2026 update made Arsenals activate after the match begins.

February 2026 introduced **Hardlight Mesh**: transparent, destructible window barriers that block shots and movement until breached. Controller characters can rebuild or reinforce them.

**Design lesson:** A battlefield can contain useful structures, routes and recoverable positions. A defender’s layout becomes more interesting when attackers can scout, breach, flank or temporarily reshape it.

## 7. Modes and their different purposes

| Mode or feature | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Core Unranked | The main ruleset without Ranked progression. |
| Ranked | Competitive play and ranked standing. |
| Wildcard | Faster, simplified battles with more permissive recovery and character-selection rules. |
| Mixtape | Rotating alternative combat formats. |
| Bot Royale Evolved | Matches with human and bot squads. |
| Training / Firing Range | Learn controls, test equipment and practise. |
| Private matches | Organised custom games. |

Wildcard generally uses around 30 players and shorter matches. It permits duplicate Legends within a squad and simplifies equipment and progression. Bot Royale Evolved supports XP and challenge progression, but ordinary badges and stats are restricted. Private matches do not award normal XP progression.

The published Mixtape rotation covering 23 October 2026 includes **Team Deathmatch, Control and Gun Run**.

**Wild Cards** are temporary gameplay perks used in Wildcard matches. They are different from collectible profile or calling cards.

**Design lesson:** Give practice, experimentation and serious competition clear rules and rewards. A successful experimental mode does not have to redefine the ranked game.

## 8. Ranked competition

Current ranked tiers run Rookie, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Diamond, Master and Apex Predator.

Ranked Points reflect placement, eliminations and applicable bonuses, with entry costs that rise by rank. Current matchmaking uses RP; hidden skill estimates help with initial and reset placement. Seasonal splits and resets affect progression.

Ranked Ladders are a separate competition using a player’s five best match RP scores within its event. Account level is a different measure from competitive standing.

A roadmap published on 20 October 2026 announces additional changes for November’s Season 31 and early 2027’s Season 32, including character bans and later rank changes. Those are future plans at the date of this brief.

**Design lesson:** Explain what a rating measures, how it changes and what resets. A leaderboard for skill should not quietly become a leaderboard for hours played or money spent.

## 9. Account progression and mastery

Account XP rewards activities such as survival, wins, combat and helping teammates. Account level records accumulated progression and is separate from Ranked status.

Weapon Mastery adds a persistent track for individual weapons, including trials and cosmetic recognition such as badges, trackers, frames and skins. Its purpose is to reward learning and repeated accomplishment with a tool.

**Design lesson:** Separate experience, competitive skill, contribution and specialisation. A strong support player, a reliable organiser and a high-ranked attacker can all deserve recognition for different reasons.

## 10. Banners, cosmetics and visible identity

Apex banners assemble several identity elements:

- A frame.
- A character pose, including animated options.
- Up to three achievement badges.
- Up to three statistical trackers.
- Introductory and kill quips.

Other customisation includes character and weapon skins, emotes, holosprays, skydive emotes, finishers and melee cosmetics. Prestige skins offer visual stages unlocked through gameplay after acquisition.

**Design lesson:** A profile becomes more meaningful when the owner chooses which achievements to display. Presentation should tell viewers who the player is and why their accomplishments matter.

## 11. Currencies, rewards and the shop

Apex has several persistent currencies with different uses:

| Currency | Broad use |
|---|---|
| Apex Coins | Premium purchases. |
| Crafting Metals | Selected cosmetic crafting. |
| Legend Tokens | Earned progression currency, including character unlocks. |
| Heirloom Shards | Prestigious collectible items. |
| Exotic Shards | Cosmetic customisation. |

Apex Packs award cosmetics and crafting-related rewards rather than direct combat-stat boosts. Access to additional Legends is a separate unlock system, and payment can provide earlier access to character choices.

**Design lesson:** Currency systems should be understandable. A new feature does not automatically need another currency. Reward rarity and visual quality can create collection value without becoming mandatory combat power.

## 12. Seasons, challenges and passes

The current Battle Pass structure has two passes per season, one per split. Free and paid options provide rewards through progression, including daily and weekly challenges. Current tiers are Free, Premium, Ultimate and Ultimate+.

Seasonal updates can introduce or change characters, equipment, maps, balance and event rules. The current patch material demonstrates why a live game’s design reference needs dates and versioned rules.

**Design lesson:** A season can provide a fresh theme, goals and recognition while players keep a meaningful history of earlier accomplishments.

## 13. Communication, accessibility and feedback

Apex offers communication and accessibility options including contextual pings, colour-vision settings, subtitles and chat accessibility features.

The Firing Range supports experimentation with equipment, movement, targets and combat practice. It gives players a place to understand systems before a competitive match.

**Design lesson:** Useful information needs more than decorative effects. Players should understand threats, ability ranges, objectives and team requests through labels, shapes, animation and sound, with alternatives for different needs.

## 15. Use one operative to lead the existing army

For the first combat version:

**One member starts one assault with one selected operative and an army built from the existing troop roster.**

The player chooses deployment, target priorities and ability timing. Ordinary units follow the existing combat behaviour. The operative uses a defined command slot, and their power is included in the overall army budget.

This fits an overhead or angled 3D tactical battlefield. The player should be able to read the base, deploy units and activate abilities comfortably on desktop or mobile.

A later two- or three-person co-op mode can let members share an operation, but that needs an explicit additional milestone for unit ownership, live networking, disconnects and shared rewards.

## 16. Six original operative concepts

These are commanders who complement the ordinary troop roles.

### Rivet — engineering and breaching

- **Passive: Prepared Entry.** A nearby breacher group gains a small benefit on its opening breach.
- **Tactical: Cutting Charge.** Weakens or opens one eligible barrier.
- **Ultimate: Breach Corridor.** Creates a short opportunity to push through a selected route.
- **Counterplay:** defenders can use layered positions and alternate firing angles; critical command structures remain protected by objective rules.

### Signal — reconnaissance

- **Passive: Field Observer.** Scouts gain better local intelligence after reaching an observation point.
- **Tactical: Survey Pulse.** Briefly reveals threats within a limited area.
- **Ultimate: Overwatch Network.** Provides temporary sector intelligence and marks priority threats.
- **Counterplay:** limited range, duration and visibility rules; expired information becomes “last seen” instead of remaining perfectly accurate.

### Remedy — medical support

- **Passive: Triage.** Makes a finite recovery resource slightly more efficient.
- **Tactical: Trauma Station.** Deploys a small, destructible healing point.
- **Ultimate: Emergency Extraction.** Recovers a limited number of eligible incapacitated allies.
- **Counterplay:** exposed healing equipment, interrupted recovery and strict per-attempt limits.

### Palisade — defensive support

- **Passive: Entrenched.** Helps a small formation hold a position under defined conditions.
- **Tactical: Cover Projector.** Deploys temporary, destructible cover.
- **Ultimate: Hold the Line.** Establishes a short-lived defensive position.
- **Counterplay:** flank, destroy the cover, attack another objective or wait out the effect.

### Switchback — movement and logistics

- **Passive: Advance Team.** Improves the repositioning of a limited detachment.
- **Tactical: Rapid Route.** Opens a temporary movement route for eligible units.
- **Ultimate: Tactical Redeployment.** Moves a limited surviving group to a legal deployment point.
- **Counterplay:** route limits, visible destinations and protected areas that cannot be bypassed.

### Cinder — assault coordination

- **Passive: Focused Pressure.** Rewards sustained engagement with a marked target within a cap.
- **Tactical: Suppressive Mark.** Helps nearby units pressure one threat.
- **Ultimate: Coordinated Assault.** Creates a brief, clearly signalled offensive window.
- **Counterplay:** repositioning, cover, defensive abilities and forcing the attacker to split attention.

**Prototype Rivet, Signal and Remedy first.** They test three different functions: access, information and recovery. Expand the roster after the ordinary army and those abilities interact reliably.

## 17. Make abilities readable and limited

Show the player each ability’s valid targets, range, area, duration, charges and cooldown before activation.

Start with:

- A modest passive.
- One tactical ability with a clear cooldown or charge limit.
- One ultimate use per attempt.

Any later charge-generation system needs valid triggers and caps. Repeatedly healing manufactured damage must not generate endless ultimate charge or mastery rewards.

Show opponents enough information to respond: a breach warning, scanner pulse, cover perimeter or recovery signal. Visual quality should make those signals clearer.

On mobile, keep the main interface compact: deployment controls, operative portrait, tactical button and ultimate button.

## 18. Separate the five progression layers

| Layer | What belongs here | Reset rule |
|---|---|---|
| Community development | Buildings, research, shared resources, approved projects. | Persistent under existing base rules. |
| Personal progression | Operative mastery, service history, earned cosmetics and cards. | Persistent. |
| War state | Sector damage, captured objectives, remaining attack budgets. | Persists within that operation; concludes under its rules. |
| Assault state | Field upgrades, local loot, shield charge and ability charges. | Ends with that attempt. |
| Competitive season | War rating, seasonal standings and seasonal awards. | Reset or archived according to published season rules. |

Call temporary combat choices **Field Upgrades**.

Keep **calling cards** as permanent identity and collection items. A rare animated card should not be required to win a competitive assault.

Within an assault, a field upgrade might offer:

- Faster repositioning or stronger temporary cover.
- A wider scan or a longer-lasting scan.
- More healing capacity or faster deployment of the medical station.

These are choices within a bounded power budget. They expire after the attempt.

## 19. Connect equipment to existing buildings

Use the existing base facilities:

- **Barracks:** troop preparation and operative selection.
- **Forge/Workshop:** equipment options and tactical sidegrades.
- **Research Lab:** unlock trees and approved improvements.
- **Communications Tower:** scouting and coordination tools.
- **Infirmary:** recovery preparation and limited support resources.
- **Vehicle Depot:** later travel, convoy and extraction options.
- **Warehouse:** shared storage and expedition readiness.

Keep Timber, Scrap, Supplies and Parts useful. Add recipes or mission rewards before introducing another currency.

For ranked wars, establish equal budgets within the format, a viable shared core of options and defined limits on equipment and operative statistics. Lock relevant research and loadouts with the operation’s rules.

PvE can expose more of the community’s accumulated progression through harder missions and broader choices. Label difficulty and reward expectations clearly.

## 20. Add cover, breaches and route decisions

Let base layouts influence the battle:

- Walls and shutters create routes that can be breached.
- High positions offer useful sightlines.
- Cover protects units but can be flanked or destroyed.
- Observation points reward scouts.
- Support stations create defensible recovery positions.
- Optional supply points compete with primary objectives for attention.

A repaired barrier must obey the operation’s rules. In shared wars, previously committed destruction and captured objectives cannot silently reverse because a later player used a repair ability.

Start with a small set of readable interactions. A wall that visibly changes from intact to damaged to breached is more useful than several visually different structures with unclear behaviour.

## 21. Give rescues clear consequences

Use three clear states: **active**, **incapacitated**, and **removed from the attempt**.

Suggested first rules:

1. Eligible units can become incapacitated.
2. A valid support unit or operative ability can recover them.
3. Recovery consumes a limited field resource or charge.
4. Each unit has a defined recovery limit.
5. Recovery restores a defined amount of health.
6. Units removed from the attempt stay removed.

The attack token is consumed when the assault starts. Recovery, surrender, disconnecting or reopening the browser does not create a new free attempt.

Use the existing Infirmary and Supplies for any post-mission readiness system. Keep recovery requirements visible before commitment, and keep ranked readiness rules consistent across matched communities.

## 22. Add useful pings before live co-op

Members should be able to place signals such as:

- Attack this sector.
- Scout this route.
- Reserve this objective.
- Supplies needed.
- I am handling this.
- Hold position.
- Extraction ready.

Every ping needs an author, target, timestamp, expiry and acknowledgement state.

Show the difference between a suggestion and an approved commander order. Allow limited reservations to expire so an absent member cannot hold an objective indefinitely.

Use text and symbols alongside colour. Share only the intelligence the viewer is allowed to see. Public spectators can receive delayed highlights instead of live private plans.

## 23. Make shared sector warfare reliable

Preserve the earlier operation structure: equal locked rosters and attack budgets, defense and research snapshots, and damage that persists within the operation.

At the start of an attempt, record the attacker, operative, troop loadout, equipment, sector state, consumed attack token and rules version.

For the first implementation, permit **one active assault per sector**. Other eligible sectors can be attacked concurrently. This prevents two attacks from committing incompatible versions of the same defenses.

If the player disconnects, continue the documented battle behaviour; reconnecting returns to that same attempt. Apply its result and rewards once.

Keep the agreed scoring priority:

1. Completed objectives.
2. Strategic destruction.
3. Fewer attacks when the previous measures are equal.
4. A draw if still equal.

Operate from authoritative battle records. Replays must show the actual result, including ability timing and structures destroyed by earlier assaults.

## 24. Use hazard and extraction missions as an optional mode

A strong DZN twist is a **salvage expedition**:

1. Enter a temporary industrial site.
2. Scout valuable caches.
3. Breach a route.
4. Choose between an extra reward and a safer departure.
5. Rescue eligible casualties.
6. Extract as contamination, fire or structural collapse intensifies.

The hazard changes the temporary mission area. Permanent community buildings retain their progression.

An extraction could require holding a beacon while a convoy arrives. The player chooses between carrying more materials, keeping additional support equipment or leaving early.

Introduce this as PvE before considering a competitive variant. Standard sector wars keep their established objectives.

## 25. Connect travel to staging

Keep the existing world map, real eligible server records, search, marker clustering, public gameplay regions and bounded logistics.

A journey should deliver an expedition to a **staging window**. An eligible player starts the active tactical mission when ready within that window.

Display separate timers for:

- Travel.
- Staging availability.
- Active mission duration.
- War deadline.
- Recovery.

Ranked wars use standardised participation windows. A convoy arriving while its owner is asleep must not automatically start an active ranked battle and produce an offline defeat.

Automated expeditions, if developed later, need their own disclosed resolution rules.

## 26. Turn calling cards into premium personal showcases

A DZN identity card could contain:

- Original operative portrait or animated scene.
- Community crest and affiliation.
- Selected mastery emblem.
- Up to three achievement slots.
- Selected verified statistical trackers.
- Seasonal frame, title and edition.
- A detail panel explaining how each achievement was earned.

Use a compact version for member lists and leaderboards, with a detailed showcase when opened.

For the highest visual tier, use layered depth, restrained animation, reactive lighting, strong silhouettes and a convincing material finish. Provide reduced-motion and lower-cost render versions so the card remains usable on mobile.

Example achievement concepts:

| Card or badge | Qualification idea |
|---|---|
| Siege Medic | Complete meaningful recoveries in eligible operations. |
| First Through | Open a successful route that contributes to an objective. |
| Eyes on Target | Supply reconnaissance used in a completed operation. |
| Last Convoy | Finish a defined high-risk extraction objective. |
| Citadel Founder | Meet a published contribution condition during a Citadel promotion. |
| Perfect Operation | Complete a specified operation under clear efficiency rules. |

Use original final names and artwork. Treat these labels as working concepts.

Each award should disclose its qualifying mode, season, criteria and evidence. Distinguish PvE records from competitive records. Imported external statistics require a reliable integration; unavailable data should show as unavailable.

If an edition is limited, publish the edition rule and preserve provenance. A display title such as **“God-tier”** should describe the cosmetic presentation or achievement tier, not hidden combat strength.

## 27. Keep recognition and competition distinct

Maintain separate views for:

- **Community war rating:** performance in eligible competitive operations.
- **Community contribution:** useful participation in construction, logistics and missions.
- **Operative mastery:** demonstrated progress with a particular commander.
- **Collection:** earned cards, frames, badges and cosmetics.
- **Service history:** significant operations and community milestones.

Do not make raw donations or time online the universal skill score.

Support and objective work need credit, but only for useful actions. Healing fabricated damage, repeating already completed objectives or transferring the same materials back and forth should not inflate achievement counters.

A personal ranked ladder can follow once DZN has a standardised personal competitive format. The existing community war ladder can come first.

## 28. Keep the reward economy consistent

Continue the existing Pro ownership and free-member participation model.

Earnable rewards can include mastery cosmetics, operation badges, card frames, titles and community decorations. Optional premium rewards can focus on additional presentation, cosmetic customisation and showcase features.

Preserve a viable competitive equipment pool for every eligible member. Scarce cosmetic ownership should not decide combat eligibility or strength.

A later seasonal service track can reward varied activity: scouting, building, logistics, rescues and objectives. Publish what expires and what remains in a player’s archive.

Start with direct achievement and crafting routes. The first implementation can function without random paid packs or a new collection currency.

## 29. Website screens and interaction design

Organise these additions around the existing DZN experience:

| Screen | Main purpose |
|---|---|
| Community Headquarters | Shared 3D base, construction, resources and contribution prompts. |
| Operations Board | Available missions, sector state, reservations and attack budgets. |
| Operative Bay | Character preview, abilities, mastery and loadout. |
| Deployment | Objective, army capacity, costs, recovery rules and expected duration. |
| Battlefield | Deployment controls, pings, abilities and readable tactical information. |
| After-action Report | Outcome, contribution, rewards and replay. |
| Personal Showcase | Calling cards, selected achievements and service history. |

Use original DZN characters, environments, sound and interface styling. Keep the premium appearance consistent across the community base and personal identity systems.

Show the next useful action clearly. For a member, that might be “contribute 20 Scrap,” “reserve a scout mission,” or “join the staged expedition.” For an owner, it might be “approve the next project.”

## 30. Example end-to-end member journey

A community completes its Forge upgrade using shared contributions.

A member opens the Operations Board and reserves a salvage mission. They choose Rivet, then assemble rifle, scout, breacher and medic units within the normal army budget.

During the attempt, scouts locate a defended route. Rivet opens a barrier, the player chooses a temporary field upgrade, and the medic recovers an eligible casualty.

As contamination advances, the player chooses an early extraction with fewer materials rather than risking the remaining army for another cache.

The server records the result once. Recovered Parts enter the community’s appropriate storage under its contribution rules. The member earns eligible Rivet mastery and progress towards a rescue or breach achievement.

Temporary field upgrades end. Permanent cards and community construction remain. The after-action report shows what happened and why each reward was granted.

## 32. Implementation acceptance criteria

Before promoting the addition into ranked community wars, verify that:

- Existing base progression, resources, server identities and ordinary troops still work.
- The operative fits within the established army budget.
- Ability ranges, limits and counters are understandable.
- Permanent cards cannot create scarce competitive power.
- Recovery cannot generate unlimited lives.
- Rejoining cannot reset an attempt or duplicate a reward.
- Shared sector damage commits once and remains consistent.
- Locked loadouts and rule versions determine competitive outcomes.
- Travel reaches staging without auto-starting an offline ranked loss.
- Mobile users can deploy units and activate abilities reliably.
- Reduced-motion presentation remains usable.
- Replays and achievement evidence match the recorded result.
